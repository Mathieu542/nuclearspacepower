Fission reactor vs
Solar + battery in orbit

For a given electrical payload in low Earth orbit, which power architecture costs fewer kilograms to launch? This canvas compares a fission reactor (thermal conversion + radiator) against a photovoltaic array plus battery storage, as a function of altitude, required power, and the technology assumptions of each pathway. Drag the sliders — it's meant to be re-tuned. The URL updates as you go, so any scenario can be shared by link.

First-order model, basic physics only (radiative balance, orbital eclipse geometry, specific masses). No proprietary data. Layout inspired by Andrew McCalip's orbital vs terrestrial data center cost analysis — the subject and the equations here are different: mass, not cost, and power, not compute.

Model assumptions

Shared

  • Circular orbit, Earth's shadow modeled as a cylinder (no penumbra).
  • The beta-angle slider is the worst case over the mission (its lowest |β|); all eclipse-driven sizing is computed there, since β drifts seasonally.
  • Launch mass only — no hardware cost, no non-recurring development cost here.
  • No fairing envelope or launch sequencing constraints.
  • No radiation degradation of structures, other than solar cell degradation.
  • No deorbit, on-orbit servicing, or regulatory compliance mass accounted for.
  • Power management & distribution (regulation + distribution downstream of the power source) is not modeled — it's common to both architectures and not a discriminating factor between them.

Nuclear

  • Reactor core is sized by thermal power, conversion equipment by electrical output — so the efficiency slider correctly moves both waste heat and reactor mass.
  • Specific powers are treated as constants (no scale effect — a future refinement), and there is no reactivity loss over mission life.
  • Shield mass is a fixed input, not derived from thermal power or altitude — a known simplification.
  • Radiator sized purely from radiative balance (Stefan-Boltzmann) at the chosen hot-side temperature; no conduction/interface gradient modeled.
  • Altitude-independent by construction — the reactor carries no eclipse penalty, which is the core of the comparison.

Solar + battery

  • The array must power the payload in full sun and recharge the battery before the next eclipse — sized for steady periodic operation at the worst-case beta.
  • Linear cell degradation; the array is oversized at beginning of life (BOL) to still hold the required power at end of life.
  • No dedicated radiator mass — assumes the array backside is sufficient, as on a Starlink-class bus.
  • Battery sized by usable energy only (depth of discharge + round-trip efficiency); cycle-life fade is not yet modeled — a future refinement.
  • Deployed panel area is illustrative only (array BOL power ÷ areal power density) and does not feed back into the mass. It is much larger than payload ÷ density because the array covers the full BOL power — payload plus battery recharge during sunlight, plus degradation margin.
